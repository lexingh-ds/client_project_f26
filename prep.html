Preparing for Your Engaged Learning Experience

Launch Your Engaged Learning Journey

Transform your academic foundation into real-world impact. Before beginning your project, take time to understand the community context, reflect on your own perspective, evaluate your team's readiness, and identify what you still need to learn.

Why Preparation Matters

Engaged learning at UMSI is different from a traditional classroom assignment. You are entering complex, real-world environments where technical solutions intersect with human lives, organizational cultures, and community needs.

Preparing thoughtfully helps you build a foundation for ethical and sustainable engagement. Before writing code, creating designs, or analyzing data, reflect on your assumptions and consider the perspectives of the people affected by your work. Approach your partner as a collaborative learner rather than as an outside expert arriving with a predetermined solution.

Preparing for the Experience

Develop Social and Community Awareness

Examine how your identity, experiences, assumptions, and areas of privilege may influence the way you understand a community or project. Learn about the community's context and consider how power and participation can affect decision-making.

Understand Expectations and Roles

Review program requirements, project commitments, leadership responsibilities, deadlines, and expected outcomes. Consider what responsibilities you and your teammates will have before beginning the work.

Begin Project Scoping

Learn about the organization's needs, intended users, constraints, and desired outcomes. Consider whether your team's skills, availability, and resources align with the project's initial needs before committing to a solution.

Questions to Consider

  • What do I hope to learn from this experience?
  • Who will be affected by the project and its outcomes?
  • What assumptions am I making about the partner, community, or project?
  • What knowledge and skills does my team already have?
  • What knowledge or perspectives are missing from our team?
  • What do we still need to learn about the partner or community before beginning the project?
  • What would a mutually beneficial outcome look like?

Featured Preparation Resources

  • "Check Yourself" Community Engagement Checklist: Quick audit for personal assumptions and ethical readiness.
  • IAP2 Spectrum of Public Participation: Framework for understanding levels of community involvement and decision-making power.
  • Project Scoping Handout & Terms: Guidance on translating partner needs into realistic project goals.
  • Student Org Officer & Project Manager Guide: Roles, expectations, and commitments for student organization leaders.
  • Project Manager Commitment Form: Formal agreement outlining responsibilities for student leads.
  • Project Management Worksheet: Guidance on clarifying the project scope and objectives, team roles, and timeline. This worksheet can be used by the project team and shared with the client.
